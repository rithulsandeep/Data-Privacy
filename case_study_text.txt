Title 
Privacy Challenges in AI-based Medical Diagnostic Systems: A Federated Learning 
Approach for Secure Disease Prediction and Public Health Surveillance 
 
Background 
Healthcare organizations generate enormous volumes of patient information every day, 
including medical history, symptoms, diagnoses, laboratory reports, prescriptions, and 
treatment outcomes. AI systems require this data to identify disease patterns and improve 
clinical decision-making. 
Traditional AI systems rely on centralized databases where patient information from multiple 
hospitals is collected in one location. Although this improves model performance, it 
introduces significant privacy and security risks. 
 
Existing System 
Traditional healthcare AI systems typically follow a centralized architecture. 
Working 
 
Hospitals collect patient data.  
 
Patient records are uploaded to a centralized server.  
 
A machine learning model is trained using the combined dataset.  
 
Predictions are generated from the centralized model.  
Limitations 
 
Raw patient data leaves hospitals.  
 
Large centralized databases become attractive targets for cyber attacks.  
 
High infrastructure costs.  
 
Privacy regulations restrict data sharing.  
 
Hospitals are reluctant to share confidential patient records.  
 
Single point of failure.  
 
 
 
 
Proposed Solution 
Proposed Federated Learning System 
The proposed system follows a decentralized learning architecture. 
Workflow 
1. Each hospital stores patient records locally.  
2. Local AI models are trained independently.  
3. Only model parameters are transmitted.  
4. Model updates are encrypted before transmission.  
5. The central server aggregates updates from all hospitals.  
6. An improved global AI model is generated.  
7. The updated model is distributed back to all hospitals.  
8. No raw patient information ever leaves the hospital. 
 
Objectives 
1) To analyse the privacy risks associated with AI-based medical diagnostic systems that 
rely on centralized collection and processing of sensitive patient data. 
2) To study how Federated Learning can be used as a privacy-preserving machine 
learning approach that enables collaborative model training without sharing raw 
patient information. 
3) To identify and evaluate privacy-preserving techniques such as encryption, 
differential privacy, secure aggregation, and access control in protecting healthcare 
data throughout the AI lifecycle. 
4) To compare centralized AI architectures with decentralized Federated Learning 
architectures in terms of privacy, security, scalability, and regulatory compliance. 
5) To examine the ethical, legal, and security implications of deploying AI-driven 
healthcare systems that process sensitive medical information. 
 
Privacy-Preserving Technologies Used 
The proposed system employs multiple privacy-preserving mechanisms to ensure secure 
collaborative learning. 
Federated Learning 
Allows hospitals to collaboratively train AI models without exchanging patient records. 
Differential Privacy 
Adds carefully controlled noise to model updates, reducing the possibility of reconstructing 
sensitive patient information from transmitted parameters. 
Encryption 
Protects model updates during transmission between hospitals and the central aggregation 
server. 
Secure Aggregation 
Ensures that the server aggregates encrypted model updates without learning individual 
hospital contributions. 
Local Data Storage 
Patient records remain within each hospital and are never transferred outside the institution. 
Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) 
Restricts access to sensitive patient information based on authorized user roles. 
Audit Logging 
Maintains records of data access and model updates to ensure accountability and traceability. 
These mechanisms are part of the proposed architecture, which also includes encrypted local 
databases, audit logging, secure aggregation, and differential privacy during federated 
training.  
 
Expected Outcomes 
The proposed system is expected to 
 
Preserve patient privacy throughout the learning process.  
 
Eliminate the need for centralized patient databases.  
 
Improve disease prediction accuracy.  
 
Provide better treatment recommendations.  
 
Detect disease outbreaks at an earlier stage.  
 
Enable collaborative learning among hospitals.  
 
Reduce privacy risks associated with centralized healthcare AI systems.  
 
Improve clinical decision-making using collective knowledge.
